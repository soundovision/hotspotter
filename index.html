HOW IT WORKS

Scan your album, or your entire discography, in seconds.

Hot Spotter was built by a producer and mastering engineer with over 2,000 credits. It's a standalone app designed to streamline the process of getting an objective view of loudness, dynamics, and tone for an album, mixtape, or full discography.

  1. 01

    Drop your mixes or masters.

    Drag the album folder onto Hot Spotter. It analyzes WAV, AIFF, FLAC and MP3s with an industry-standard engine (integrated LUFS, EBU R 128), all on your machine. No cloud, fully functional offline.

  2. 02

    Read the room.

    Every track is measured against your album's own average, or a reference or target you set. Tracks that drift louder or quieter get flagged for your review, and the tinted waveform shows exactly which sections do it.

  3. 03

    Fix mistakes, lock in intention.

    Bounce a new master or confirm a track is how you like it, then hit Analyze Again. Every change is tracked in History, so you can watch your album's levels take shape.

OVERVIEW

Loudness & Tone

Hot Spotter is a unique loudness meter that focuses on loudness and balance across as many tracks as you can throw at it. Perfect for albums, mixtapes, single series, an entire discography.

Traditional loudness meters focus on A/Bing, comparing two tracks to one another. Hot Spotter expands on this idea, allowing artists, producers, and engineers to create a consistent sound across a full collection of music.

Wait, but Why?

Algorithms don't listen to your music, they watch user behavior. Is your song's mix or master turning off listeners?

In the era of streaming, passive listening, and playlists, having a consistent loudness and tone across an album or catalog is critical. Listeners skip tracks that are too quiet or too compressed, and streaming platforms' algorithms impose penalties.

Genre Advantage

For artists that create lo-fi, ambient, acoustic, or classical music, having a consistent loudness and tone across your whole album can vastly increase the performance on streaming of your project.

For artists with dynamic genres, like jazz, EDM, and rock, you can now pinpoint which tracks should be your loudest and have the most energy, and ensure that they punch above their weight in the context of your album and against your references.

TRIM

Trim the Fat

In traditional metering apps, you'd have to edit, re-export, and re-analyze a track with a long intro or outro to get a clear loudness reading. Use the Trim feature in Hot Spotter to instantly adjust the readings to focus on the core of your song.

Use keyboard shortcuts to rapidly Trim as many tracks as you want across your album. Then A/B the trimmed versions against the full tracks to see things lock in.

QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Created by a DJ & producer to streamline music production workflows.

What does Hot Spotter measure?

Per track: integrated LUFS, true peak (dBTP), loudness range (LRA), short-term loudness over time, dynamics (PSR), and a five-band tonal balance (sub, bass, low-mid, high-mid, high). Loudness and tonal balance are shown against the album's own average.

Per album: mean loudness, loudness spread, and a full change history between runs.

Do my tracks need to hit −14 LUFS?

Good gravy, they don't!

−14 LUFS is a playback normalization number, not a mastering order. Hot Spotter shows you whether your album is consistent with itself. Does track five jump out of bounds? Is that intro too quiet, and will it get people to skip?

If you do want a fixed number, click the album average and type one; it becomes your target. Or load up your favorite album or track and use it as a reference.

What formats does Hot Spotter analyze?

WAV, AIFF, FLAC and MP3. If a folder mixes MP3s with lossless files, Hot Spotter asks which formats to analyze, as lossy formats like MP3 can meter louder than lossless formats like WAV.

If you have MP3s alongside lossless versions, no worries: a WAV+MP3 pair of the same song is detected and shown as one track.

Is my audio uploaded anywhere? (Are you scrapin' my data??)

Absolutely not! Hot Spotter is a native desktop app, and analysis runs on your machine. The app only goes online to check for updates, to activate or deactivate your license key, and, if you turn them on, to send anonymous usage reports. Those never include your audio, file names or measurements. See exactly what's sent.

How does the free version count tracks?

The free version analyzes 20 tracks on each computer. A track is a file name inside an album folder, counted once: re-exporting a master to the same file name is free, renaming it or moving the folder counts as new, and a WAV+MP3 pair of the same song counts once.

References never count, and neither do failed or cancelled runs. Free tracks are used first, then pack tracks. Your reports and history stay readable after your tracks run out.
