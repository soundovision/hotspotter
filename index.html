HOW IT WORKS

Scan your album, or your entire discography, in seconds.

  1. 01

    Drop your mixes or masters.

    Drag the album folder onto Hot Spotter. It analyzes  WAV, AIFF, FLAC and MP3s with an industry standard engine (Integrated LUFS, EBU R 128) all on your machine. No cloud, fully functional offline.        

  2. 02

    Read the room.

    Every track is measured against your album's own average, or a preset target. Full tracks or small sections that drift louder or quieter get flagged for your review.

  3. 03

    Fix mistakes, lock in intention.

    Bounce a new master or confirm a track is how you like it, then re-run the analysis. Every change is tracked with a change-log, so you can watch your album's levels take shape.

NO PRESET POLICE

A loudness meter for producers, indie musicians, and DJs. 

Hot Spotter doesn't grade you against a streaming target or a broadcast spec. Load a reference of your favorite song or album, input a manual target you think sounds better, or make all your tracks your own. 

By default, Hot Spotter's reference is the record you're making: every measurement is a distance from your album's own average. Adjust the slider from left to right to get your tracks tighter or more dynamic. You decide between intentional dynamics or slamming consistency.  

For those who'd rather chase a tried and true reference, you can manually input one of your own, or load a reference file or album to get a picture of what your peers and idols are doing.

QUESTIONS & ANSWERS 

Created by a DJ & Producer to streamline music production workflows   

What does Hot Spotter measure?

Per track: integrated LUFS, true peak (dBTP), loudness range (LRA), max short-term loudness, dynamics (PSR), and a five-band tonal balance (sub, bass, low-mid, high-mid, high). Each value scanned is  shown against the album's own average.
Per album: mean loudness, dynamics spread, and a full change history between runs.

Do my tracks need to hit −14 LUFS?

Good gravy, they don't! 

−14 LUFS  is a playback normalization number, not a mastering order. Hot Spotter shows you  whether your album is consistent with itself. Does track five jump out bounds? Is that intro too quiet and will get people to skip? 

If you do want a fixed number, click the album average and type one; it becomes your target. Or load up your favorite album or track and use it as a reference. 

What formats does Hot Spotter analyze?

WAV, AIFF, FLAC and MP3. Mixed-format albums get a warning banner and per-track format chips, as lossy formats like MP3 can meter louder than lossless formats like WAV. 

If you have MP3s alongside of lossless versions, no worries, a WAV+MP3 pair of the same song is detected and shown as one track.

Is my audio uploaded anywhere? (are you scrapin' my data??)

Absolutely not! Hot Spotter is a native desktop app, analysis runs on your machine, and the app makes no network requests except checking for updates. License keys are checked once and the app never phones home.
